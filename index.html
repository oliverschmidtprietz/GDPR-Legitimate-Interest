§ 01 · CAPABILITIES

SIX PRIMITIVES.
One SKILL.

Each capability is documented separately, tested separately, and called by the workflow at the right moment. Compose them or invoke individually.

01
PRE-ASSESSMENT GATES
Four threshold exclusions run before the test: G1 public-authority bar, G2 ePrivacy lex specialis, G3 national-law restriction, G4 post-consent fallback. Any gate can take Art. 6(1)(f) off the table.
02
THREE-STEP TEST
EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 methodology: legitimate interest (lawful / clearly articulated / real and present), strict necessity, and the balancing test — each with a PASS / FAIL / REFINE verdict.
03
REASONABLE EXPECTATIONS
Recital 47 and C-252/21 Meta analysis: existing relationship, collection context, and the rule that common practice ≠ reasonable expectation, anchored to OSS enforcement patterns.
04
MITIGATING MEASURES
When the balance tips against the controller, add measures beyond GDPR obligations — extended erasure, unrestricted objection, pseudonymisation — then re-run the balance (paras. 57–58).
05
CONTEXTUAL MODULES
Specialised guidance for AI/ML training and web scraping, direct marketing, fraud prevention, IT security, employee monitoring, children, and the ePrivacy overlap.
06
ART. 21 OBJECTION
Right-to-Object mode applies the higher 'compelling legitimate grounds' threshold (essential, not merely beneficial) against the absolute Art. 21(2) marketing opt-out.
§ 02 · WORKFLOW

FROM INPUT
to ARTEFACT.

Ten structured steps. The human stays accountable; the skill carries the structure, the citations, and the document trail.

01
Disclaimer & mode
Non-blocking disclaimer (not legal advice; route to DPO and counsel; document under Art. 5(2)). Choose a mode: Full LIA, Quick Check, Legal Basis Comparison, or Right-to-Object.
02
Recency search
Search current enforcement when the scenario touches AI / web scraping, a named jurisdiction, a cited CJEU case, or UK / DUA Act 2025 processing.
03
Pre-assessment gates
G1 public authority · G2 ePrivacy · G3 national-law restriction · G4 post-consent fallback. A failed gate stops the assessment before the three-step test begins.
04
Information gathering
Adaptive conversational intake against a nine-field coverage checklist; group follow-ups; mark unresolved fields as cautious assumptions and flag them in the output.
05
Step 1 — legitimate interest
Three cumulative criteria (lawful, clearly articulated, real and present; EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 para. 17). Classify controller vs. third-party interest. Verdict: PASS / FAIL / REQUIRES REFINEMENT.
06
Step 2 — necessity
Strict-necessity test (C-26/22 SCHUFA, para. 88): less-intrusive alternatives and data minimisation per category. 'Necessary' ≠ 'useful'. Verdict: PASS / FAIL / REDUCE SCOPE.
07
Step 3 — balancing
Weigh the interest against data-subject rights: nature of data, impact, and reasonable expectations (Recital 47; C-252/21 Meta, para. 117). Verdict: PASS / FAIL / PASS WITH MEASURES.
08
Mitigating measures
If the balance tips against the controller, add measures beyond GDPR obligations and re-run the balance (paras. 57–58) before concluding.
09
Contextual & cross-border
Load the relevant module; flag retroactive-reliance risk (rejected by most SAs) and the possible need for localised LIAs where Member State law diverges.
10
Documentation
Produce the LIA: a summary card and/or the full 12-section .docx with a legal-references annex and DPO / counsel sign-off, ready for accountability records.

§ 10 · README.MD

Legitimate Interest (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f) LIA) — Deployment Guide

GDPR Legitimate Interest Assessment skill for Claude — guides a structured Art. 6(1)(f) assessment using the EDPB three-step test and produces a documented LIA suitable for accountability records.

Overview

Combines the current legitimate-interest sources into a guided three-step workflow:

  • EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 on Art. 6(1)(f) (adopted 8 October 2024) — the core interpretive document
  • EDPB Opinion 28/2024 on AI models (17 December 2024) — AI-specific LIA guidance
  • EDPB OSS Case Digest on Legitimate Interest (Dr. TJ McIntyre, March 2026) — 62 OSS decisions + 5 EDPB binding decisions
  • CNIL Recommendations on Legitimate Interest for AI Development (19 June 2025) + web-scraping focus sheet
  • UK ICO Legitimate Interests Guidance (updated 23 March 2026) — incl. the DUA Act 2025 "Recognised Legitimate Interest" basis and the divergent necessity standard
  • CJEU case law and key context modules (direct marketing, fraud prevention, IT security, employee monitoring, AI training, web scraping, credit checks)
  • Jurisdiction coverage — EU, UK, FR, DE
